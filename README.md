# CVGAN_V2

Earth System Models like atmosphere-ocean coupled climate models (AOGCMs) attempt to simulate the worldwide climate for both historical periods and the future, under various forcings. While these models often represent the variability of many climatic variables very well, but they are incapable of representing the underlying processes fully and accurately, resulting in spatial and temporal biases in their simulations. Furthermore, the time and computational complexity required for each simulation are very high. The rapid advances in AI models for weather prediction open the possibility of applying similar data-driven models to better emulate climate variability and improve climate predictions. AI-based generative models are capable of synthesizing new realistic data, making them suitable for such emulation. This work introduces Climate Variability Generative Adversarial Network (CV-GAN) - a generative model based on Pix2Pix Conditional Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) to emulate global SST variability. We aim to use it to generate worldwide SST data more efficiently and accurately than AOGCMs by fine-tuning with observational data to remove the biases. In our experiments, we use SST data from 1 random ensemble generated from each of the three GCM models MIROC6, EC-Earth3-CC, GISS-E2-1-H model (under CMIP6 family), followed by fine-tuning with observational data from the COBE  dataset. A 500-year simulation by CV-GAN is found to overcome major biases of AOGCM simulations by simulating SST variability significantly closer to observations. The proposed CV-GAN model shows high uncertainty with similar variability patterns, confirming its ability to emulate a diverse ensemble of anomaly datasets with similar long-term variability patterns.

 The CV-GAN is also trained on two other AOGCMs to confirm that the proposed approach is effective in correcting the major biases of the AOGCMs. These results indicate that CV-GAN has the potential to be used for seasonal to multi-decadal climate simulations similar to AOGCM with significantly less computational requirements.
